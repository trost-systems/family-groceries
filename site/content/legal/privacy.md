# Privacy policy

This is a translation. The [German version](/datenschutz) is authoritative.
Last updated: 14 November 2026

This page explains which personal data Snacklist processes, why, on what legal basis, who receives it and what rights you have. Snacklist is a shopping-list app for households on iPhone, Android and in the browser (app.snacklist.app), with the website snacklist.app.

## 1. Controller

Peter Trost, Yalovastr. 5, 72108 Rottenburg am Neckar, Germany. E-mail: [support@snacklist.app](mailto:support@snacklist.app). No data protection officer has been appointed.

## 2. Website snacklist.app

The website sets no cookies, loads nothing from other servers and uses no analytics. The pages are served as static files. Only the waitlist form sends your details to our server, a function at Vercel (section 2a). The connection is received by the Vercel location nearest to you. Vercel is a US company; access from the USA is possible, and the transfer relies on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and Standard Contractual Clauses (section 13). When you visit it, our host Vercel Inc. (440 N Barranca Ave #4133, Covina, CA 91723, USA) processes technically necessary data: IP address, time, page requested and browser identifier. This is needed to deliver the site and protect it from attacks (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; our legitimate interest is secure operation). Server logs are kept for at most 30 days.

## 2a. Beta waitlist

If you join the waitlist on snacklist.app, we store your email address, whether you have an iPhone or an Android phone, the language of the page (German or English), whether you also want an email when the app launches, the times you signed up and confirmed, and the version of the consent text.

Why: we send you an email to confirm your address, then a welcome email and the invite to the Snacklist beta. Only if you ticked the box do we also email you once when the app officially launches. We need your phone platform to invite you the right way, and the language to write to you in German or English. For the invite we pass your email address to Apple (TestFlight) or Google (Google Play); Apple or Google then sends you the invite (section 12).
Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).

Confirmation: you are only on the list once you tap the link in the confirmation email. The link works for 48 hours. We store only the time, not your IP address. If you don't confirm, we delete the sign-up after 7 days.

If an AI assistant or someone else enters your address for you, we only send the confirmation email. You're only on the list once you agree yourself on the confirmation page. Until then we keep your details (address, phone platform, language) based on our legitimate interest in checking that the address really is yours (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) and delete them after 7 days; the delete link in the email does it right away. We don't store who or what entered the address.

We invite in weekly groups: everyone who confirmed in the same calendar week. We start each invite by hand; there is no automated selection.

Service providers: the form is received by our host Vercel; the server function runs in Frankfurt am Main, and Vercel sees your IP address as with every visit (section 2). Sign-ups are stored at Supabase in the database in Frankfurt am Main. Emails are sent by Resend from Ireland; Resend stores the emails and delivery logs in the USA and deletes them after 30 days (sections 12 and 13). Our emails contain no tracking pixels, and links are not redirected to count clicks.

Unsubscribe and objection: every email has an unsubscribe link; one click is enough, no sign-in. You can also write to support@snacklist.app. This withdraws your consent for the future and objects to the use for these emails (Art. 21(2) GDPR); we delete your sign-up at once.

Retention: without the launch email, we delete your sign-up 3 months after the beta invite is sent; with it, 30 days after that email is sent, and in any case by 31 December 2027.

Proof of consent: so that we can show you agreed, we keep a record after the end: a check value of your email address from which the address can't be read, the version of the consent text, the language and the times of sign-up, confirmation, last email and unsubscribe. We delete it after 3 years. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR with Art. 7(1) GDPR, and Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in being able to prove consent).

The waitlist and the beta are for people aged 16 and over.

## 3. Web app app.snacklist.app

The web app is also delivered by Vercel as static files (see section 2).
Snacklist stores the following in your browser's local storage or on your device: your sign-in session; a copy of your lists and your list settings, so the app starts quickly and works offline; on iPhone and Android, a copy of your list so Home Screen widgets can show it, even if you have not set up a widget: the names, quantities and stores of your first six open items and the number of all open items, in storage on the device shared by the app and the widget; if you sign out, the app replaces it; which of your households you last opened on this device; an invite you opened, until you join (section 5); your language; your choice about usage data (section 9); your answers during setup while you have no account yet (who you shop with, the household name, stores and your first items), so setup continues after a restart, until signing up turns them into your household and list and the copy on the device is deleted (if you choose "Discard progress", it is deleted too); and which tips you have seen or hidden and how often you used certain features, so tips only come when they help (this never leaves the device). These are strictly necessary for the app you asked to use (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). A service worker caches the app files. A random installation ID is stored only if you allow usage data (section 9).

## 4. Account and sign-in

For an account we process your e-mail address, a password (stored only in hashed form) or your Google or Apple sign-in, and a display name. If you sign in with Google or Apple, we receive your account ID, your e-mail address (with Apple possibly a relay address) and, if provided, your name; Google or Apple learn that you sign in to Snacklist. Sign-in links and confirmations are e-mailed via our service provider Supabase, which sends them through our service provider Resend (section 12).
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (performance of the user agreement).

## 5. Shopping list and household

You can be a member of several households. We store what you enter in Snacklist: the household name, who you shop with (the kind of household: partner, family, flat share, club or team, just me or other) if you say so during setup or later, members, their roles and when they joined, invitations, lists (name, order, who created them and when), items (item name, quantity, list, who added and bought it, timestamps), a history of changes (which item was changed, by whom, whether it was added, ticked off, restored, edited or deleted, and when), and what Snacklist learns for your household (categories, stores, brands). What you type yourself under "Other" stays on your device and is not stored on our servers. A household can have several lists. If a member deletes a list, we delete its items with it. All members of a household see its lists and the other members' display names and roles. The app does not currently show the history on any screen, but the members of the household can access it. We use it for suggestions, and the changes you made are included in your account data export. The members of a household don't see which other households you are in. You choose one of your households as your default; until you choose another, it is your first household. Siri, Alexa and AI assistants work in your default household (section 7).
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

With "I'll bring it" you say on an open item that you will bring it, with an amount if you like (for example "2 cakes"). We store who brings which item, the amount and when. All members of the household see this with your display name. The claim ends and we delete it when you take it back, the item is ticked off or deleted, its list is deleted, you leave the household or are removed, or you delete your account.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### Roles and invites {#invites}

Whoever creates a household is its owner and can make other members admins. The owner and admins can remove members from the household; an admin can't remove the owner or other admins. If the owner leaves the household or deletes their account, the role passes to the admin who has been in the household longest, otherwise to the member who has been in it longest.

When the owner or an admin removes a member, we store who was removed from which household and when. For 30 days this keeps the person from coming back through an invite created before the removal (after that, those invites have expired) or through an invite from a member who isn't an admin. During that time they can only rejoin through a new invite from the owner or an admin. The removed person sees a notice about it in the app; the household's other members don't see the record. We delete it after 30 days, when the person rejoins through such a new invite, with their account or with the household. If you were removed, the record is in your export (section 8).
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (the household's legitimate interest that a removed member doesn't come back through old invites).

Snacklist doesn't send invites. You share a link or code yourself, for example through a messenger. Any member can create a link for one person; it expires after 7 days and works only once. The owner and admins can also create a group link; it expires after 30 days, works for up to 50 joins and the owner and admins can revoke it at any time. For each invite we store who created it, when it expires, how often someone joined through it and how often its join page was opened. That is only a number; we don't store who opened the page. When someone joins, we store through which invite and when. The household's members see this under "Who joined". If someone leaves the household or is removed, we delete this record.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

The owner and admins can choose to confirm joins ("Confirm new members"). For households of the kind club this is on from the start. An invite then doesn't make you a member straight away; instead we store a join request: who wants to join, which household, through which invite and when, and later the decision (approved, declined or withdrawn by you), when and by whom it was made. Only you and the household's owner and admins see the request, with your display name; the other members don't see it. An open request stays until it is decided, you withdraw it or you join the household another way. If your request was declined, you can ask this household again only after 24 hours; for this we use the stored time of the decision. We delete decided requests after 7 days in a daily clean-up.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

An invite link opens a join page of the web app (section 3). Anyone who has the link sees there, even without an account, the first name of the person who invited (only if they entered a display name), the household's name, the number of members and open items, and whether the household has Snacklist Premium. When you create an invite, the app tells you this. The page never shows item names, e-mail addresses or IDs. So only pass a link on to people you want to invite. So that nobody finds invite codes by trying them out, we limit how often the page can be queried per hour. For this our server stores, per hour, a check value of the request's IP address and a check value of the code (each a SHA-256 hash with a secret added value) with the number of requests. We don't store the IP address itself. We delete an hour's check values after the hour has ended. Vercel delivers the page.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our legitimate interest in letting invited people see what they are invited to before they join, and in nobody finding invite codes by trying them out).

If you tap "Paste invite", the app reads the clipboard once and takes only an invite link or code from it. Without that tap, Snacklist doesn't read the clipboard.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR; for reading from your device, § 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG.

### Snacklist Premium in the beta {#premium-beta}

For testers and for Apple's and Google's review of the app, we unlock Snacklist Premium free of charge; the unlock lasts until we end it. You buy nothing and pay nothing, and the unlock never turns into a paid purchase or subscription. For this, we create your household at our processor RevenueCat, Inc. (USA, sections 12 and 13) with the household's ID (a random number, not your account ID) and give it the free unlock there with a start and end date. RevenueCat receives only this ID and the details of the unlock, no names, e-mail addresses, account IDs or list content; because we do this and not the app, it doesn't receive your IP address or device data either. Our server asks RevenueCat which unlocks the household has and stores only that a free unlock exists, since when and until when. When RevenueCat reports a change, we store only the report's ID and type, for 90 days. The household's members see in the app that it is unlocked free of charge. If anyone in that household sends usage data (section 9), we mark it as internal so it is left out of our statistics.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our interest in testing the unlock under real conditions before sales start, giving testers Snacklist Premium free of charge, having the app reviewed by Apple and Google and preventing misuse). You can object at any time (section 15); we will then end the free unlock for your household and have its record at RevenueCat deleted.
Retention: we delete the entry on our side when the unlock ends or we end it, at the latest with the household. We delete the household's record at RevenueCat 30 days after the household is deleted.

## 6. Voice input

When you dictate an item in the iPhone or Android app, your operating system's speech recognition turns speech into text. This happens only on your device: if the device cannot recognise German without the internet, Snacklist does not offer voice input. There is no voice input in the browser. The app sends the recognised text to our server, which turns it into items; we never receive audio.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

## 7. Siri, Alexa and AI assistants

You can connect Snacklist to AI assistants (for example through the Model Context Protocol at mcp.snacklist.app) or to Siri through a shortcut you set up yourself. This only happens if you create an access token or approve a connection yourself. For Siri directly in the iPhone app, see "Siri on iPhone" below. The connected service can then read and change your list items, and its provider processes that content under its own privacy terms. An access token works only for the household you created it in. An approved connection works in your default household (section 5). It also sees the names of your other households and their number of members, and can read and change their items if the service names one of these households, for example because you ask it to. For each token we store a name, a hash, its last four characters and when it was last used. For each approved connection we also store a short device label such as "Chrome · macOS", which your device derives from the platform and browser; it contains neither the full browser identifier (user agent) nor the IP address. It is shown under More › Access as "Approved on Chrome · macOS" and is deleted with the connection. When a token ends, for example because you revoke it or leave the household it works for, we delete this data at the next daily clean-up 30 days later. You can revoke tokens and connections in the app at any time.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### Siri on iPhone

When you are signed in to the iPhone app and belong to at least one household, the app automatically creates its own access token for Siri; you don't need to do anything. In the app it is called "Siri (this iPhone)". The token is kept in this iPhone's Keychain and is not transferred to other devices. For it we store a hash (SHA-256) of the token, its last four characters, the name "Siri", which sign-in session it belongs to, and when it was created and last used. The token only works for Siri requests to Snacklist, and only while you are signed in and a member of at least one household. It always works in your default household (section 5); if you choose another default, it works there from the next request.

When you ask Siri to add something to your list or read it out, Apple turns your speech into text. We do not receive a voice recording. We only receive the recognised text or the name of a suggestion you picked (for example "milk"), whether you want to add or hear items, the language (German or English) and the token. When reading out, no text is sent. We add the items to your default household's list just as if you had entered them in the app; the other members see them. We do not write the text to logs or to usage analytics; for errors we only log the type of error.

So that Siri understands phrases like "Add milk to Snacklist", the app stores up to 50 item names of the household you currently have open in the app, each with up to 2 further spellings from the same household, on your iPhone and passes them to iOS's App Shortcuts. How Apple processes your speech, its transcript and this data is governed by Apple, as an independent controller, in its privacy policy and in your iPhone's Siri settings; we have no influence on this.

The token ends when you sign out, leave your last household, delete your account, revoke it under More › Access, or sign in again on this iPhone (the app then creates a new one). Each account has at most five Siri tokens; when another is added, the oldest ends. After a token ends, we keep its stored data for 30 more days so we can look into misuse or questions, then delete it at the next daily clean-up. If the token ends because you signed out, we notice this by the next day at the latest.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

### Amazon Alexa

You can enable the Alexa skill "Snacklist" in the Alexa app and link it to your Snacklist account. To do so, you sign in to Snacklist and allow "Amazon Alexa" to access your list. Amazon receives an access token and a refresh token for this and keeps them.

When you ask Alexa to add something to your list or read it out, Amazon records your speech and turns it into text. We do not receive a voice recording from Amazon, only the recognised text (for example "milk and eggs"), the kind of request, the access token, an ID of your Alexa account and technical details about the device and language. We do not use the device ID. We add the items to the list of your default household (section 5), just as if you had entered them in the app; the other members see them. In our reply we send Amazon the item names, which Alexa reads out to you and shows as a card in the Alexa app: the items just added, or up to 10 open items on your list.

For this we store a hash (SHA-256) of your Alexa ID, which sign-in session belongs to it and when you last used the skill, so that we can end the connection when you disable the skill. We also count how many items you added in the current minute (at most 30). We delete this data as soon as the connection ends: when you disable the skill in the Alexa app, link it again, choose "Disconnect Amazon Alexa" under More › Access, or delete your account. If you don't use the skill for 12 months, we end the connection and delete this data as well. If you only unlink in the Alexa app, Amazon deletes the tokens; the entry under More › Access stays until you remove it or it has gone unused for 12 months.

Amazon processes your voice recordings, their transcript and our replies as an independent controller under Amazon's Alexa Terms of Use and privacy notice. For Alexa on amazon.de, your contract partner is Amazon Media EU S.à r.l., 38 avenue John F. Kennedy, L-1855 Luxembourg. How long Amazon keeps voice recordings and how you delete them is set in the Alexa app; we have no influence on this.
Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR.

## 9. Usage data and error reports (only with consent)

Only if you agree, Snacklist stores a random installation ID on your device and sends usage data and error reports to our processor PostHog Inc. (servers in the EU, Frankfurt). Sent: the installation ID, your household's ID, platform, app version, technical details of the device and the app (in the iPhone and Android app: app build, device maker, model and type, operating system and version, screen size, language, time zone, whether Wi-Fi or mobile data is used, on Android also Bluetooth status, the mobile network's name and the system's HTTP user agent; in the browser: browser and operating system with version, device type, screen and window size, language and time zone, but never the page address or the referring page), a random session ID that changes after 30 minutes without use, actions such as "app opened for the first time", "household created", "invite accepted", "item added/ticked off", "category/store reordered", "household switched", "member removed", "list created", "list switched", "I'll bring it", "join requested", "join request decided", "setup finished", "tip shown/hidden/used", "invite sent", "item categorised", "duplicate-item hint shown/merged/ignored", "checklist step done", "checklist dismissed", "feedback submitted" and "review prompt requested" (with details such as channel, category or screen, as well as further details such as your role in the household, the number of members, whether an item was typed, dictated, suggested or added through Alexa, the kind of invite, how you joined (through a link, through "Paste invite" or through "Join with a code"), where a household was created, whether a join request was approved, who you shop with as a choice such as "family", how many stores and items were set up, and which tip it was), and error reports. We clean errors in the app's own code before sending. If the iPhone or Android app crashes, it sends the operating system's crash report (error type, error message, technical stack trace) at the next start; we don't clean this one, but it normally holds none of your list content. For in-app purchases our servers send the purchase event (purchase, refund or restore) with the same installation and household ID, with product, price, currency, store, country and a pseudonymised purchase reference (hash), but without payment data; this too only if you agreed. If you add an item through Alexa that was not yet on the list, our servers send the event "item added" marked "through Alexa", with your role and the number of members, under the installation ID of the device on which you last decided about usage data, and only if that decision was yes. If you ask Siri to add an item or have the list read out, our servers send the events "Siri item added" or "Siri list read out", even if the list is empty or the request fails, with the result, the reason (for example added, nothing understood or text too long), the language, whether the request came through the app's own Siri connection or through a shortcut with an access token, and a count (when adding, how many items Snacklist understood; when reading, how many open items are on the list; never which ones), likewise under the installation ID of the device on which you last decided about usage data, and only if that decision was yes; they do not send the text of your request or item names. To make this possible and to be able to prove your consent, we store for your account, per installation, your decision (yes or no), when you made it, which version of this notice you saw and the installation ID: for a yes the new one, for a no the previous one, if there was one, so that we can match your withdrawal to that ID and send nothing more under it; at most the 20 most recent decisions. If we change the notice to cover new purposes or data, we ask you again and send nothing until you answer. They are deleted with your account (legal basis: Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR in conjunction with Art. 7(1) GDPR). Never sent: item names, list contents, names, e-mail addresses, IP address and location. PostHog creates no person profile and does not derive a location from the IP address. The data is pseudonymous. Also only with your consent, the app asks PostHog, using the installation ID, which features are switched on for your installation. For this, in the iPhone and Android app, the PostHog software stores the installation ID, events not yet sent and the answer about switched-on features on your device; in the browser it stores nothing. If you switch usage data off, we delete this on your device.
Purpose: to see which features are used, find bugs, measure whether Snacklist pays for itself and switch new features on step by step.
Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR; for storing the ID, § 25(1) TDDDG). You can withdraw it any time under More › Usage data, with effect for the future.
Retention at PostHog: 1 year.

Separately, our servers report errors in server functions to PostHog. Such a report contains the name of the server function, the kind of error, a shortened and cleaned-up error message and the places in the program code where the error occurred. It contains no user, household or installation ID, no e-mail addresses, no list contents, nothing from the request and no IP address. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (legitimate interest in error-free operation). Retention at PostHog: 1 year.

## 14. Retention

We keep your data as long as you have an account. When you delete your account, we delete your account, profile, memberships, the record of which invite you joined through, your "I'll bring it" claims, join requests and stored removals, access tokens, connections (Alexa included) and your usage-data decisions immediately; for purchases we remove the link to your account. We delete a household's record at RevenueCat 30 days after the household is deleted (section 5). We also delete Alexa connection data as soon as the connection ends (section 7). If you are the last member of a household, the household and everything in it is deleted, as is any household you archived. You can also leave single households without deleting your account. If you leave a household or are removed, we delete the record of which invite you joined through and your claims in that household. With a household we also delete its lists, invites, join requests, stored removals and the record of who joined through the invites. If you leave a household with Snacklist Lifetime as its last member without deleting your account, the household and its data are deleted. Snacklist Lifetime ends with the household. Otherwise the items, lists and history stay for the other members, without any link to you; the item names remain visible. We keep shared household data, the history included, as long as the household exists, archived households included. We delete the join page's check values after the hour has ended, decided join requests after 7 days, and the record that someone was removed from a household after 30 days (section 5).
