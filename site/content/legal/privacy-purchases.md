# Privacy: buying Hummlist Lifetime

Draft. Once Peter approves it, this text becomes section "5a. Buying Hummlist Lifetime" of the [privacy policy](/en/privacy) and this page goes away. Until then Hummlist sells nothing.

## 5a. Buying Hummlist Lifetime

In the iPhone and Android apps you can buy Hummlist Lifetime, once, for your whole household. You buy it in Apple's App Store or on Google Play. Apple or Google handle the payment and process your payment and account data as independent controllers under their own privacy policies; Apple acts as commissionaire, Google as the merchant of record. We receive no payment data and no name of your Apple or Google account, and the email address of your Google account only if you give it to us for a refund request (see below).

For in-app purchases we use RevenueCat, Inc. (USA) as our processor (sections 12 and 13). Only when you start a purchase or restore a purchase on the "Hummlist Lifetime" page does the app connect to RevenueCat. RevenueCat then receives your household's ID (a random number, not your account ID), your IP address, platform, app and SDK version and language setting, and for a purchase also the store's receipt or purchase token, the product, price, currency, country and purchase date. RevenueCat receives no names, e-mail addresses or account IDs. So that the store can match the purchase to your household, RevenueCat passes your household's ID to Apple, or a hash value made from it to Google, when you buy. RevenueCat checks the purchase with Apple or Google and reports it to our server at Supabase in Frankfurt am Main; the server function that receives these reports always runs in Frankfurt. We then store: the store, the product, the store's transaction number, whether it was a test purchase, when it was bought and, if so, refunded, the price, currency and country, which account made the purchase and which household it unlocks. Until RevenueCat's report arrives, for 30 days at most, we remember which account bought for which household. We keep the IDs of the reports for 90 days so that none is processed twice. All members of your household see that the household is unlocked and whether it bought Hummlist Lifetime more than once, but not who bought it. Anyone with a valid invitation to your household sees before joining whether it is unlocked. When you join a household, we check whether you bought Hummlist Lifetime yourself in the last 30 days, so we can help you with a refund; no one but you sees this. If Apple or Google refund the purchase, including a Google Play refund we initiated ourselves, we learn this through RevenueCat, and the unlock ends. If, after the 48 hours in which Google itself refunds, you ask us to refund a Google Play purchase, we process your message like any support request (section 10). For this we ask you for the order number or the email address of your Google account and use it to find the order in the Google Play Console. There we see the order number, date, status and history of the order, the product, price, tax, total, our estimated revenue and the country of the billing address, as well as your other orders with us, but not your name, email address or payment method. If we refund, Google carries out the refund. As soon as you start or restore a purchase, the RevenueCat library stores your household's ID and the unlock status on your device; this is strictly necessary for the purchase you started (section 25(2) no. 2 of the German TDDDG).

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (contract: the purchase, restoring it and the unlock you bought, including the processing at RevenueCat); for the transaction number, the check value (see retention), the report IDs, what invitees see, the check when you join and refund requests to us also Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our interest that a purchase is not used twice or in other households, that invitees know what to expect, and that we can help with refunds and decide on refund requests); for our bookkeeping records Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR.

Testers: if you test Hummlist for us, we can unlock households for you free of charge. For this our server stores your account ID, how many households you may unlock this way and how many you have unlocked, when we added you and a short note (for example "TestFlight group"), but no name and no e-mail address. For a household unlocked this way we note that the unlock came from you; the other members only see that it is unlocked. If anyone in that household sends usage data (section 9), we mark it as internal so it is left out of our statistics. Nothing goes to RevenueCat. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR (our interest in rewarding testers and preventing misuse). We take you off the list when you have given all your unlocks or your test phase ends, at the latest when you delete your account; then we also delete the note on the household. The unlock stays with the household. Test purchases in Apple's and Google's test environments cost nothing, are marked as test purchases and are treated like other purchases.

Retention: the unlock stays with the household, also when you leave it or delete your account. When you delete your account, we remove the link between the purchase and your account; the purchase stays stored with the household. Apple or Google can still link it to a store account through the transaction number, so it is pseudonymous, not anonymous. When the last member leaves a household with Hummlist Lifetime, they can archive it instead of deleting it. An archived household has no members, and its invitations no longer work; we keep it with everything that belongs to it (name, list, history, categories, shops, unlock and purchase). Only the person who archived it sees it under "More" › "Archived households" and can re-enable it there. If it is not re-enabled within 24 months of archiving, we delete it; you can see the date there. When you delete your account, we also delete the households you archived. If you choose "Delete" instead of archiving, we delete the household and everything in it right away. When a household is deleted, its unlock ends; 30 days later we delete the purchase on our side and the household's record at RevenueCat. We delete refund requests to us like other support emails (section 10). So that the same purchase cannot then unlock another household, we keep only a non-reversible check value of the transaction number (created with a secret key), with no link to the household or to any account. Because Hummlist Lifetime can be restored at Apple and Google without a time limit, we keep this check value for as long as we sell Hummlist Lifetime or honour existing unlocks. We review every year whether this is still needed, and we delete all check values at the latest three months after we have stopped both. For our bookkeeping we receive statements, payout reports and invoices from Apple and Google. For each purchase they contain the order number, country, product and amount, and in Google's case the device model, but no name. We keep these records for eight years from the end of the calendar year (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR in conjunction with section 147(1) no. 4, (3) and (4) of the German Fiscal Code (AO) and section 14b(1) of the German VAT Act (UStG)). We do not need the purchase data in Hummlist itself for this.

Your purchases are part of "Export my data". Usage data about purchases (section 9) is sent only with your consent.
