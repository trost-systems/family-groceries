## 2. Digital tool use by Vereine

### Adoption (evidence is thin)

- ZiviZ-Survey 2023: about two thirds of organisations say they are well set up digitally in
  public relations and "Koordination von Mitgliedern/Engagierten"; gaps in digital offers for
  target groups. Only 21 % gained participants through digitalisation, 9 % gained under-30s;
  18 % say digital work weakened the sense of community.
  https://e-pflicht.ub.uni-duesseldorf.de/download/pdf/414516 (ch. 9.2)
- WhatsApp: "gut 80 Prozent" of sports clubs use WhatsApp as a communication tool, per a
  Landessportbund knowledge portal citing a 2022 study on sports-club communication during
  COVID-19 (primary study not read; sample unknown):
  https://bsb.vibss.de/vereinsmanagement/digitalisierung-im-sportverein/kommunikation/soziale-medien/grundlagen/nutzung-von-sozialen-medien-in-deutschland?type=123
- Share of clubs using dedicated club management software: **unknown** (no representative
  figure found; the 2021 "Digital-Report" by Reppmann/Edinger-Schons is cited by VIBSS guides but
  I could not read its numbers). Estimate: a minority, probably 15-30 % of all e.V., reasoning:
  73 % have no paid staff, 2/3 have < 100 members (Excel/WhatsApp suffice), and the largest
  German-language vendor counts are in the low thousands (below).

### Which agents can even reach Hummlist's MCP server

- Claude: custom remote MCP connectors on all plans incl. Free (Free: one custom connector):
  https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-get-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-MCP
- ChatGPT: per this repo's `docs/MCP.md` (reading OpenAI Help article 12584461 on 2026-10-31),
  full MCP with write tools only in Business and Enterprise/Edu workspaces; Pro read/fetch only;
  Free/Plus none. Third-party pages disagree on Plus; OpenAI's help page is the authority:
  https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12584461
- So the dominant tool (ChatGPT, 71 % of German AI users) cannot write to a Hummlist list on the
  consumer plans club volunteers typically use; Claude (5 % of AI users) can, for free.

### Estimate: share of club organisers who would manage a list through an agent over MCP

Estimate, per club (one organising person per club):
- uses AI at all: ~65 % (Bitkom 58 % population; DSEE 65 % of pure volunteers, self-selected so high)
- uses it weekly or more: ~35-45 % of all organisers (Bitkom 34 % population; DSEE 43 % of respondents)
- uses an agent that can write via a custom MCP connector (Claude any plan, ChatGPT Business/Enterprise,
  other MCP clients): ~5-10 % of those (Claude 5 % share; few volunteers are in ChatGPT Business
  workspaces, though some use their employer's)
- willing to add a custom connector with OAuth for a club list: ~25-40 % of those
- Result: **~0.5-1.5 % of club organisers today**, i.e. roughly **3,000-9,000 of 629,000 Vereine**
  (estimate). This would rise sharply if ChatGPT opened write MCP to Plus/Free or if Hummlist
  were listed in a client's app directory.

## 4. What a club needs from a shared list app

Product needs (from the tools above, club size data and GDPR guidance; my synthesis):
- **Roles**: Vorstand/organiser (admin) vs. member; admins create lists, invite and remove people
  (Spond, Vereinsplaner and ClubDesk all model admin vs. member).
- **Scale**: dozens to hundreds of members per club (2/3 of clubs < 100 members, 13 % > 300; ZiviZ 2023).
- **Several lists**: one per event (Sommerfest, Weihnachtsfeier), one standing list for the
  Vereinsheim kitchen/bar, one per team for supplies.
- **Who brings what**: claim an item, quantity needed vs. promised (Spond: "how many people are
  needed" per task; potluck tools: who brings what and when).
- **Join without friction**: link or QR in the existing WhatsApp group; many members will never
  install an app (Spond and BringList both lean on low-friction joining).
- **Privacy between members**: under GDPR, members are third parties to each other. The LfDI
  BW guidance (hosted by BayLDA) says members may not simply get other members' data (lists, notice
  boards, password-protected web pages) unless the club's purpose, set in its Satzung, requires it
  (Art. 6(1)(b)) or a balancing test allows it (Art. 6(1)(f)); lists should hold only the contact
  data needed, with a right to object. => show display names only, never emails/phone numbers.
  https://www.lda.bayern.de/media/info_bw_verein.pdf (section 5.1)
- **Legal basis**: club may process members' data needed for the membership relation and club
  aims under Art. 6(1)(b); other purposes need Art. 6(1)(f) or consent. Same document, sections 2.1, 4.1.
  Sachsen-Anhalt LfD FAQ: consent for third-party purposes:
  https://datenschutz.sachsen-anhalt.de/fileadmin/Bibliothek/Landesaemter/LfD/Informationen/Internationales/Datenschutz-Grundverordnung/FAQs_zum_Datenschutz_in_Vereinen.pdf
- **AVV (Art. 28)**: moving member data into a cloud member service is processing on behalf;
  it needs a binding contract under Art. 28(3); non-EU processors need Chapter V safeguards
  (LfDI BW guidance, sections 3.2-3.3, same PDF). Club software vendors publish AVV templates
  (easyVerein above; DFBnet: https://intg-portal.dfbnet.org/fileadmin/content/downloads/DFBnet_Verein/DFBnetVerein-Finanz_Auftragsverarbeitung-DSGVO.pdf).
  => Hummlist would need a click-through AVV for clubs, a TOM list, a sub-processor list
  (Supabase, Vercel, PostHog, and the AI provider question below) and EU hosting.
- **Function-based access**: each officer sees only the data the role needs (LfDI BW 4.1).
- **Information duty (Art. 13)** at collection and **deletion** when a member leaves (LfDI BW 1.3, 6):
  => remove member, delete or anonymise their entries, export for the club.
- **Data protection officer**: needed only if ≥ 20 people regularly process data automatically
  (§ 38 BDSG since Nov 2019) or Art. 37 cases; rarely applies to small clubs:
  https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bdsg_2018/__38.html
- **AI agent caveat (my inference, not from a regulator)**: when the organiser's agent reads the
  list, list contents (names of who brings what) flow to the AI provider under the organiser's
  account. The club is controller for that; a regulator view on this for clubs is **unknown**.
  Keep list data free of member contact data and let members stay pseudonymous (display names).
- **Export**: CSV/print of a list for the event day; club-level export on request (Art. 15/20).

Pattern: these tools spread **one organiser -> one link -> many participants without accounts**.
The participant rarely converts into an organiser of their own list; growth is bounded by the
number of organisers, not participants. Club-wide apps (Spond) spread top-down through the club's
admin and then team by team. Evidence that club members carry a club tool into their household:
**unknown** (no data found).

## Verdict on the club niche

- **Size**: large on paper: 629,000 e.V. (March 2026), 86,000 sports clubs with 29.3 M memberships,
  ~27 M volunteers; most clubs are small (2/3 under 100 members, 73 % without paid staff) and free-tool
  minded (WhatsApp ~80 %, Spond free, ClubDesk free under 50 members).
- **Reachable share today is small** (estimate): ~0.5-1.5 % of club organisers both use an
  MCP-capable agent with write access and would add a custom connector, so ~3,000-9,000 clubs in
  Germany. The bottleneck is ChatGPT: 71 % of German AI users use it, but write MCP needs a
  Business/Enterprise workspace; Claude (5 % share) works on Free. No club tool offers MCP, so the
  slot is open, but there is no evidence of demand (no survey asks about agents).
- **What must be built** before a club pilot: roles (admin/member) and removing members; many
  members per group; several named lists per group; claim/assign items with quantities
  ("Kuchen 3 of 5 promised"); join by link without seeing other members' emails (display names
  only); export; a click-through AVV, TOM and sub-processor page; MCP tools scoped to a chosen
  list. Today's model is one household with one list, so this is a real product change, not a toggle.
- **Competition**: Spond's free event tasks cover "who does what" for clubs already on Spond;
  free one-link Mitbringlisten (BringList, SignUpGenius-style) cover one-off events. Hummlist's
  edge would be the grocery strengths (categories, aisle order, live sync, voice/Siri/Alexa) plus
  the agent, for the standing Vereinsheim kitchen/bar list more than for one party.
- **Risks**: GDPR duties shift onto Hummlist as processor (AVV, deletion on exit, member-to-member
  data rules); member data flowing into the organiser's AI provider; viral loop is weak (participants
  of a bring-list rarely become organisers; household spread from a club list is unproven); clubs pay
  little or nothing; support load from volunteer admins; a ChatGPT policy change could open or keep
  closed the main channel.
- **How to test cheaply**: (1) no build: a landing page section "Vereinsheim-Liste mit KI" plus a
  waitlist question "Which AI do you use: ChatGPT Free/Plus, Business, Claude, none?", posted where
  club organisers gather (e.g. digital verein(t), LSB KI workshops, r/de clubs) and measure sign-ups
  per 1,000 views; (2) concierge pilot: 3-5 clubs use today's household as a "club household" for
  one event or the clubhouse kitchen, organiser connects Claude; watch whether members join by link,
  whether anyone asks for roles/claims, and whether any member starts their own household
  afterwards; (3) count, via existing analytics, how many current households already have >4 members
  or event-like lists, as a proxy for demand.
- **Overall**: a good story and an open niche, but too small and too ChatGPT-gated to be a
  growth engine now; worth a cheap demand test, not a roadmap commitment, until write MCP reaches
  ChatGPT consumer plans or a pilot shows member-to-household spread.
