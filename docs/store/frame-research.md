# Store frame research for Hummlist (job 77)

Date of research: 2026-11-02. Purpose: inform five frame proposals for the
App Store and Google Play screenshot sets of Hummlist (German shared grocery
list; honey yellow #ffe24a, cobalt #1b3fae, ink #0f1530, hexagon motif,
tagline "mitsummt"). The job brief allowed a bumblebee mascot, but Hummlist
has no mascot artwork and the 6b design rules rule out drawing a bee
(DECISIONS, design 6b; the hummlist-ui-craft skill), so the proposals use
none. Where this file discusses a mascot, read it as the case for one.

How to read the evidence. Apple and Google pages are rules. ASO vendor blogs
are mostly the vendors' own claims. Where a number has no named study behind
it, this file says so. No source found a controlled test of device frame
versus frameless, and none found a rigorous public test of panoramic layouts.

## 1. How competitors frame their store screenshots

Method: I pulled the German App Store screenshot URLs through Apple's public
iTunes lookup and search API (country=de) on 2026-11-02, downloaded the first
two to four frames of each app and looked at them. I did not see Google Play
sets. Descriptions below are of the iPhone German listings as served that
day. Sets change often. We describe only and do not copy art.

### AnyList (blue, device, big caption)

- Flat saturated blue background, one full iPhone with a black bezel
  centred, caption on top.
- Caption is white, bold, about 40 px high on a 220 px wide thumbnail, two
  lines, 5 to 6 words ("Listen mit Familie und Freunden teilen").
- Frame 1 states the sharing value. Frame 2 is "Erstelle Einkaufslisten und
  Listen aller Art". Frame 3 is recipes. No mascot, no panorama.
- The 9 frames use one template, so the set reads as a series.

### Bring! (mint, illustrated, two phones)

- Teal-mint background with flat fruit illustrations (strawberry,
  blueberries, paper plane) that bleed in from the corners.
- Frame 1 shows two tilted phones, one dark and one light, plus two circular
  portrait photos of people and a floating red "Milch" tile that leaves the
  phone. The tile pulls the product's own UI object out of the device.
- Caption top left, white, bold, 3 lines, left aligned ("Die einfachste
  Einkaufsliste zum Teilen."). Frame 2 centres a 2 line caption.
- Phone is cropped by the frame edge on frame 2. Shows the sharing
  story with people, not a mascot.

### Todoist (soft cream and peach, laurels first)

- Pale peach background with a soft gradient. Phone is tilted and cropped at
  the bottom edge. Frame 1 has award laurels at the top (NYT Wirecutter,
  Apple Today "App des Tages"), then a dark caption ("Erfasse Aufgaben in
  Gedankenschnelle").
- Frame 2 has a small illustration of a hand holding a card, and two UI
  cards floating out of a cropped, dimmed phone with a bottom sheet. The
  floating cards are the callouts. Caption 4 to 5 words, mid weight, ink.
- Detail UI is enlarged beyond the phone. Not panoramic.

### Things 3 (blue, device, sentence captions)

- Plain blue background, a tall device cropped at the bottom, captions at the
  top left in white, 2 to 3 lines, sentence style ("Konzentriere dich auf den
  heutigen Tag. Zeige Kalender-Ereignisse und Aufgaben zusammen an.").
- Bold lead phrase then regular text. 15 to 20 words, so long for a
  thumbnail. No mascot, no illustration. The app's quiet tone is kept.
- Strict, calm, one template. Rely on the app's brand and its Editors'
  Choice badge rather than on the frame.

### OurGroceries (purple, product photo cut-ins)

- Saturated purple (magenta toward lilac) background, big white bold caption
  at the top, 2 to 3 lines ("Lebensmitteleinkauf leicht gemacht!", "Mit der
  ganzen Familie teilen").
- Phone is cropped at the bottom edge, strawberries photo placed over the
  lower corner of frame 1. Frame 2 shows two overlapping phones with a pale
  curved arrow between them, which is a literal sync diagram.
- Pre-2020 look, but caption size is the largest of the set I looked at.

### Structured (pink, panoramic hints, awards)

- Light pink background. Frame 1 holds a stack of laurels (Apple Design
  Award 2026, Forbes, Editors' Choice, App of the Day) and a short caption
  ("Dein Plan für jeden Tag"), with a phone entering from the right edge.
- Frame 3 shows the phone tilted and cropped at the left edge, so frames 1
  and 3 look like a continuous strip. Frame 2 ("Routinen leicht gemacht")
  has a small phone above a large cut-out UI control (day chips, weekly
  toggle) as a callout.
- Captions are 3 to 4 words, large, ink on pastel. Closest of the group to
  panoramic plus callouts.

### Listonic (green, uppercase, callout card)

- Two tone green. Frame 1 has laurels, then a heavy uppercase caption in
  green on a pale mint background ("PLAN' MEHR, ZAHL' WENIGER") and a
  grey sub line. Frame 2 is solid bright green with white uppercase caption
  and a sub line.
- Device is a simple thin bezel. One UI row ("Zwiebel", checked) is
  lifted out as a white callout card that overhangs the phone.
- Captions are 4 to 5 words with a sub line, so about 9 words per frame.

### Patterns across the seven apps

- All seven keep a device frame. None is frameless full bleed.
- Phone is cropped at the bottom edge in five of seven. Callout cards or
  lifted UI appear in Bring!, Todoist, Structured and Listonic.
- Captions sit at the top in all seven. Colour is white on saturated or ink on
  pastel.
- Caption length ranges from 4 to 6 words (AnyList, Bring!, Todoist,
  OurGroceries, Structured) to about 18 words (Things 3).
- Two of seven use illustration (Bring! fruit, Todoist hand and card). None
  uses a brand mascot in the frame. A mascot is open space for Hummlist.
- Award laurels lead frame 1 in Todoist, Structured and Listonic. Google
  Play forbids award and ranking claims in feature graphic text, and in
  Play screenshots treat the same rule as applying.
- Sharing with people is the headline of frame 1 in AnyList, Bring! and
  OurGroceries. It is the category's core value, and it is Hummlist's.
- Panoramic: only Structured hints at it, and no one runs a full strip.

## 2. Published best practices

### 2.1 What Apple says

- Up to 10 screenshots per device size. Focus each on one main benefit or
  feature. Include Dark Mode if supported. Localize per market.
  (Apple product page guidance.)
- Search results show the first one to three screenshots, depending on
  orientation, when no app preview exists. "Make these highlight the essence
  of your app." (same page.)
- Previews autoplay muted, up to 30 seconds, up to three per page. A preview
  replaces the screenshots in the search result, so the poster frame matters.
- Screenshot files: jpeg or png, no alpha, one size is enough if the UI is
  the same across sizes: 1290 x 2796 and 1320 x 2868 are accepted for large
  Dynamic Island iPhones, 1179 x 2556 or 1206 x 2622 for the required medium
  class. (App Store Connect screenshot specifications.)
- Apple gives no caption length rule and no panoramic rule.

### 2.2 What Google says (Play Console help, "Add preview assets")

Screenshots:

- 2 minimum, 8 maximum per device type. JPEG or 24-bit PNG, no alpha.
- Each side between 320 and 3840 px. The longer side may be at most twice
  the shorter side (2:1 maximum).
- For featuring eligibility, apps need at least 4 screenshots at 1080 px or
  more: portrait 1080 x 1920 minimum (9:16), landscape 1920 x 1080 (16:9).
  Tablets and Chromebooks need at least 4 and use 16:9 or 9:16, 1080 to
  7680 px.
- Use captured in-app footage. Put UI in the first three. Stylised and
  multi-image layouts are allowed.
- Taglines must not exceed 20% of the image and must be localised. Avoid
  small text and calls to action.
- Clean status bar: no notifications, full battery, Wi-Fi and cell icons.
- No Play or other store badges, no device imagery, no unlicensed trademarks,
  nothing blurry, stretched, skewed or upside down. No fingers on the
  device.
- Alt text up to 140 characters.

Feature graphic:

- 1024 x 500 px, JPEG or 24-bit PNG, no alpha.
- Keep logo, name, slogan and main UI toward the centre, out of cutoff
  zones. Keep only background elements at the edges.
- Use vibrant colours. Avoid pure white, black or dark grey, which blend into
  the Play background.
- Echo the icon's style but do not duplicate the icon. Avoid fine detail.
- No device imagery, no store badges, no third-party characters or logos.
- No "Best", "#1", "Free", "Sale", awards, testimonials, price or promo
  words.
- Localise the text. Alt text up to 140 characters.
- When a promo video is linked, the feature graphic is the video cover and
  a play button overlays it. Google's page does not say where. A vendor guide
  says the middle (AppLaunchFlow), another page says some sources put it
  bottom-right, so keep the centre and bottom-right empty of key text.
- The promo video must be a single public or unlisted YouTube URL with ads
  off. First 10 seconds should show real use. Only the first 30 seconds
  autoplay, muted.

Safe zone numbers. Google gives no pixel zone. Vendors differ: one says a
924 x 400 frame with 50 px padding, another 716 x 350 centred. Plan for the
tighter 716 x 350 centre zone.

### 2.3 ASO vendor evidence

Caption length:

- AppTweak: short, benefit driven, bold and clear captions. No character
  count. Example: "Track your workouts effortlessly" rather than "Workout
  tracker app".
- AppRadar: captions under 10 words, benefits not feature names.
- Sonar (vendor blog, no cited source): 3 to 8 words, 3 to 5 the sweet spot.
  A caption that wraps at thumbnail size is too long. Headline minimum 72 px
  on a 1290 px wide canvas, subhead 48 px, body 36 px. Search cards render
  at roughly 40% size, so 72 px becomes about 29 px. These sizes are the
  author's own testing.
- SplitMetrics scroll depth study: keep captions short and legible,
  especially in search results where portrait images appear small.
- ZiMAD case (SplitMetrics): shorter, action focused captions plus a main
  character on every frame and mixed art and real UI gave a 32% conversion
  lift. Examples: "Challenging and rewarding gameplay" became "Birds
  challenge you!". The test changed several things at once, so the caption
  share is unknown. The mascot idea is relevant to Hummlist.
- Mandala Apps: caption font and position changes gave 6% (SplitMetrics
  search summary, not opened).

First three frames:

- Apple (above): first one to three shown in search.
- AppTweak: "The first three screenshots have the biggest influence as users
  rarely scroll past them." Brand insignia subtle in frame 1 if known.
- SplitMetrics scroll depth: for 5 image sets only about 15% (landscape) and
  11% (portrait) of users scroll through all 5. So about 85% to 89% never
  reach frame 5. Among those who did, conversion was about 18% (landscape)
  and 13% (portrait). Put the essence first, build a story with consistent
  captions.
- AppRadar: "Most store visitors never scroll past the third screenshot."
- Sonar cites Storemaven benchmarks (2023): about 3 seconds average first
  impression and 60% never scroll past it. I did not open the Storemaven
  report.

Panoramic and connected frames:

- No rigorous public test found. One vendor guide states that the "+30%"
  claim for panoramic layouts traces to blogs that cite no study. A vendor
  glossary says a panorama spans three frames but counts as three separate
  frames, and it removes the freedom to give each frame its own hook.
- Practical effect: in search results only 1 to 3 frames show, so the
  connection is often invisible there. It shows only after the product page
  opens and the user swipes.
- Apple has no panoramic rule. Google Play has no seamless join either, and
  its gaps differ by device.

Device frame versus frameless:

- No controlled test found. AppRadar says Google discourages device frames in
  phone and tablet screenshots (Google's own page forbids "device imagery",
  and fully prohibits frames for Wear OS). Its claim that a bezel can
  shrink visible UI by a quarter at thumbnail size is the author's.
- A vendor blog advises captions above or below the device rather than
  over the UI so the UI stays visible.
- Competitors above all use frames on iOS. Frameless is therefore a way to
  differ, with the Google rule as the safer path on Play.

Colour and contrast:

- AppTweak: bright, high contrast designs see higher conversion. Poor text
  contrast drives users away.
- SplitMetrics Paper by FiftyThree: multicolour screenshots beat monochrome
  blue and blurred brown backgrounds, and raised scroll through. Redesigned
  set gave 33% more installs overall. Localised Chinese frames beat English
  by 33%. Several changes at once.
- Sonar suggests 4.5:1 contrast (WCAG) and previewing at 40% scale.

Other published lifts (all vendor case studies, mixed changes):

- Rovio, Angry Birds 2: portrait beat landscape by 13% (AppTweak guide).
- Prisma: 12.3% then 19.7% after more tests (AppTweak guide).
- ŠKODA LittleDriver: winning iOS variant plus 15% (SplitMetrics search
  summary, not opened).
- Descriptive captions beat no caption by 25 to 30%: Sonar cites a
  SplitMetrics report it does not link. Unverified.
- Retesting quarterly versus yearly, 20 to 30% higher conversion (AppRadar,
  citing Appalize, unverified).

Localisation:

- Apple and AppTweak: localise screenshots, not only the store text.
- German and French captions run about 20 to 30% longer than English (Sonar,
  AppLaunchFlow). Size caption areas at 120 to 130% of English width. Hummlist
  writes German first, so draft in German and fit English later.
- Google: taglines and feature graphic text must be localised.

## 3. Takeaways for Hummlist

1. Frame 1 states the value in one line: shared list, everyone sees it
   live. All three grocery competitors with a sharing headline do this. Do not
   open with a feature name, a laurel or the brand name.
2. Design for the first three frames. Apple shows one to three in search, and
   about 85% to 89% of visitors never reach frame 5 in the SplitMetrics
   study. Frames 1 to 3 carry value, shared sync and speed or voice. Rank
   the rest by importance, so that frames 4 to 8 can be cut without loss.
3. Caption length: 3 to 6 words, at most 2 lines at the German length. Hard
   cap 8 words. A caption that needs a third line at 40% scale is too
   long. Sub lines are optional and only for the product page.
4. Caption size: at least 72 px on a 1290 px wide canvas (about 5.6% of the
   width), sub line at least 48 px. Weight bold or heavier. At the 40%
   thumbnail scale that is about 29 px.
5. Contrast: ink #0f1530 on honey #ffe24a, or white on cobalt #1b3fae, both
   comfortably above 4.5:1. Never put honey text on a white background.
   Check frames at 40% scale before sign-off.
6. Write the German caption first and test that it fits with 30% spare
   width. English is shorter, so it fits. Keep umlauts and long compounds
   ("Einkaufsliste", "Wocheneinkauf") in the test strings.
7. One idea per frame, as Apple says. Pull one real UI object out of the
   phone as a callout (a checked item, a member avatar), as Bring!, Todoist
   and Listonic do, instead of adding text. Use real UI, not mock data.
8. A recurring character helped ZiMAD (32% lift, mixed changes), and no
   competitor has a mascot in frames. Hummlist has none and its design rules
   rule out drawing a bee, so the proposals repeat a brand element instead
   (the hexagon, the honey colour, the member avatars). A mascot would need
   real artwork and a design decision first.
9. Keep one template across the set: same caption position, same
   background family, same margins. Vary the colour per frame in the brand
   palette only, since multicolour beat monochrome in the FiftyThree test.
10. Play: UI in the first three screenshots, portrait 1080 x 1920 minimum
    (we should export at 1290 x 2796 or larger, aspect no wider than 2:1),
    24-bit PNG without alpha, text well under 20% of the area, no device
    imagery on Play (a frameless or bleed-cropped phone is the safe cut), no
    "best", "#1", awards or free claims anywhere.
11. Feature graphic 1024 x 500: tagline "mitsummt" in the centre
    716 x 350 zone, only background hexagons toward the edges, nothing
    important in the centre or bottom-right where a play button could sit.
    Honey or cobalt ground, not white or dark grey. Do not repeat the icon
    as is. No text claims.
12. Export the Apple set once at the largest size and let App Store Connect
    scale. Re-render for Play at its limits. Include a Dark Mode frame (Apple
    recommends it) at position 6 or later. Then run a Product Page
    Optimization test of the first three frames and keep the winner. Test one
    change at a time (AppTweak).

### Frame directions: good, bad, evidence

- Panoramic strip. Good: a story that flows across the swipe, strong on the
  open product page, and a hexagon motif can run through all frames. Bad:
  invisible in search (1 to 3 frames), frames cannot be reordered, each
  frame must still work alone, and frame 1 must carry the value without the
  join. No public test supports a lift ("+30%" has no study behind it).
  Only Structured comes close among competitors.
- Bold oversized captions. Good: wins at thumbnail size and in search, the
  evidence for short, big captions is the strongest (AppTweak, SplitMetrics
  scroll study, ZiMAD). OurGroceries and Listonic use it. Bad: German length
  forces smaller type or three lines, and the UI shrinks. Keep to 3 to 5
  words.
- Tilted or floating device. Good: depth and motion, a tilt lets the UI
  overlap the caption area and hold callouts as Bring! and Todoist do. Bad:
  a tilt shrinks the readable UI, and Google calls skewed screenshots a
  quality problem and discourages device imagery on Play. Use a tilt only
  on the Apple set, or crop the bezel.
- Frameless full bleed with callouts. Good: the largest UI area at
  thumbnail size, fits the Play rules best, no competitor does it so it is
  distinct, and callouts carry the one idea. Bad: no unit of "phone"
  for the eye to anchor on, status bar and edges need care, and the
  frames can look like plain screenshots without strong captions. No test
  found for frameless against framed.
- Story sequence. Good: a narrative (list builds, partner adds, shopping
  together) uses the consistent-caption story that SplitMetrics found in the
  best SEGA variant, and suits a couples and family product and the mascot.
  Bad: the payoff comes late, and 85% to 89% never see frames 4 or later, so
  the first three must work as a story on their own. Put the hook in
  frame 1.
